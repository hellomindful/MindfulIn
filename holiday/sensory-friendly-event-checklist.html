Sensory-Friendly Holiday Event Checklist

20 practical steps for a holiday party, open house or program that is easier for guests who get overwhelmed. Print this page or save it as a PDF.

Before the event

  • Name the event’s busiest moments (arrival, food line, gifts, performances) and plan a calmer option for each
  • Pick a quiet room or corner away from speakers and the main entrance
  • Send a short “what to expect” note: schedule, noise level, lighting, food, where to take a break
  • Share photos of the entrance and quiet space ahead of time
  • Offer a quieter arrival window or a sensory-friendly first 30 minutes

The quiet space

  • Soft seating, low or warm lighting, no music
  • A small basket of fidgets and a few pairs of noise-reducing earmuffs
  • A clear sign at eye level, plus arrows from the main room
  • Water and a simple, familiar snack
  • A staff member or volunteer who knows where it is and checks in quietly

The main room

  • Keep music below conversation volume and away from food and seating
  • Turn off strobe or flashing lights; avoid heavy fragrance
  • Mark a clear path from the entrance to the quiet space and restrooms
  • Post a simple visual schedule near the door
  • Keep one exit route uncluttered and easy to find

Your team

  • Brief staff on three phrases: “Would you like a quieter spot?”, “Take your time”, and “You can come back anytime”
  • Let guests skip lines, games or photos without explanation
  • Name one point person for access questions
  • Have a plan for a guest who is overwhelmed: space, calm voice, no crowding
  • Ask for feedback afterward with one simple question: “What would make this easier next time?”
